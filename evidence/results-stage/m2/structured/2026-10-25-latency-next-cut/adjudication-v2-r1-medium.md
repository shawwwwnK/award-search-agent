## Admitted: stronger example versus a false choice argument

The answer's principal comparison is J8 versus J14: an earlier Aeroplan economy
arrival versus a much later departure and slightly shorter elapsed journey. It
then introduces “a distinctly different, LAX-positioning possibility if a
separate-ticket itinerary or business class is worth considering.” Its exact J101
economy and J43 business examples share the January 15 06:18 departure and January
17 08:50 arrival. The answer explicitly states:

> The business cabin does not buy that schedule improvement—the economy version
> is available on the same schedule.

It compares 275,000 versus 110,000 Turkish miles within one program and explains
the shared positioning fare, traveler/seat uncertainty and separate-ticket risks.
Thus it supplies the materially different cabin/booking discussion missing from
the V2 low answer. This distinction is why the present pass_minor does not reverse
[the V2 low composition-failure adjudication](adjudication-v2-r1-admitted-low.md).

The original review correctly identifies J27/J159 as stronger schedule examples:
they have the same SFO 06:18 departure and observed USD 86 positioning quote but
reach Bangkok at 04:15, 4 h 35 m before J101/J43. The source also preserves their
same 299-minute separate-ticket LAX transfer and the same program-level points
amounts. Choosing the slower pair without explaining why is an editorial weakness;
using the earlier pair would make the discussion more useful.

However, the answer does not call J101/J43 the earliest, shortest, best or only LAX
alternative. It states exact differences for that identified pair, and its cabin
conclusion remains true when J27/J159 are considered: economy can supply the same
schedule as business, while business changes the reported cabin and points quote.
J8 already provides the earliest arrival in the full saved eligible pool. The
omitted LAX pair therefore does not erase the main arrival choice or disprove the
stated reason to consider business.

This differs from the earlier material failure that claimed the extra business
points purchased an earlier arrival when an eligible economy option supplied that
very schedule. It also differs from omitting every materially different cabin and
booking alternative. Here the reviewer is asking for a better representative of an
already explained alternative. The accepted contract calls for meaningful choices,
relevant alternatives and supported rationales; it does not require an exhaustive
frontier or an optimal representative for every described family. The omission
should remain visible as a choice-strength weakness, without silently adding that
stronger requirement to the pass criterion.

Independent source checks reconcile the stated 11 h 30 m J8/J14 departure gap,
11 h 25 m arrival gap and five-minute elapsed difference. J14's 1,175-minute
duration is the minimum in the source pool. J101 arrives one hour before J14,
departs 16 h 57 m earlier and takes 15 h 57 m longer. The two Aeroplan fee quotes
are 10,740 CAD minor units, or CAD 107.40. Quote scopes remain unknown. The final
resolved document retains an unchecked fastest-journey claim; this adjudication
confirms that claim against source rather than upgrading the mechanical checker.

“Direct-award” remains potentially ambiguous traveler language for connecting
single-award journeys. The answer does not assert nonstop travel. This is a wording
improvement, not an additional material source contradiction.

## Incomplete: disclosed mismatch versus an invented workable connection

The answer opens by saying there is no complete SFO–Bangkok award itinerary to
recommend and that the summaries “don’t establish a workable itinerary.” It
accurately presents A3 as a January 1 SFO–BKK Alaska economy lead. For A110 it
explicitly says:

> It departs from Taipei a day before your January window

The reference repeats “December 31—not a complete SFO–Bangkok option,” and the
surrounding prose identifies absent schedule information, unknown price scope,
separate positioning and an unestablished protected connection. The final passage
also discloses incomplete requested-route award-detail acquisition and partial
Taipei searches. No dated SFO–TPE connection is fabricated or asserted to work.

The original review is correct that A110 cannot complete an unchanged January SFO
departure and is less relevant than in-window business leads A125/A137. The
opening conditional about prioritizing a business segment over the requested route
would be clearer if it also explicitly required changing the departure window.
Simply arranging positioning does not resolve the already disclosed date mismatch.
This is an avoidable relevance and wording weakness.

Nonetheless, the decisive date/origin mismatch is stated beside the lead, and the
answer does not claim it satisfies the unchanged request. This is materially the
same boundary as the earlier
[incomplete candidate adjudication](adjudication-r1-incomplete-candidate.md) and
[original incomplete-low review](../2026-10-25-latency/review.md#incomplete-low).
Those treated an expressly out-of-window Taipei lead as a minor issue when no
usable connection was invented. Applying a material failure solely because more
relevant leads exist would change that criterion. The source's January 1 SFO–TPE
A275 and January 2 TPE–BKK A137 do not contain schedules establishing a connection;
they are better research directions, not omitted complete journeys.

A3's 42,500 Alaska miles and 5,090 USD minor units support the stated USD 50.90
fees. A110's 30,000 Aeroplan points and 7,530 CAD minor units support CAD 75.30;
the text does not compare those points numerically with Alaska miles. C1 is the
observed USD 640 January 1 cash itinerary, arriving January 4 after 2,680 minutes
(44 h 40 m). Unknown scopes are explicit. The phrase “one-traveler search echo”
in the award-lead passage would be more precise as “the search requested one
traveler”: returned-traveler evidence is absent for that award summary. The answer
does not use the phrase to claim supported traveler coverage or per-person pricing.
The resolved document has no mechanical findings.

## Method and limits

Read both final previews, resolved selections/findings, the original source
artifacts, the initial source reviews and the existing composition criteria and
precedents. Python source inspection checked exact aliases, quote units, traveler
and schedule fields, compared the omitted LAX variants, and recomputed the numerical
differences. No summary, timing, usage or attempt-receipt files were read. No live
calls or product changes were made; only this adjudication was written.

These judgments distinguish useful but improvable examples from material falsehoods
or missing decision dimensions. They do not establish owner presentation acceptance,
repeatability, traveler benefit or milestone qualification. An owner may prefer a
stricter relevance threshold; report the concrete weaknesses and disagreements so
that preference can be applied explicitly rather than inferred from latency.
